# Knowledge Enrichment Pipeline Design

> Historical design record. Original date: 2026-08-07. Original status: Approved
> design, pending written-spec review. This is a substantive future architecture,
> not evidence that its gates, migrations, budgets, delivery, or recovery controls
> are implemented. Existing security policy is not changed by this archive.

## Problem, goals, and sequence

Snippet-based news, URL-only technical deduplication, manual dictionaries, weak
research-plan propagation, and model-reported counts limited dependable meeting
preparation. The proposal aimed for self-contained, source-grounded knowledge rather
than a link directory.

The intended order was knowledge-grade news; living technical knowledge plus
dictionaries; daily digests; then research/preparation/architecture briefs. The first
implementation plan was to cover Phase 1 only. Later phases were not prerequisites
for completing every earlier feature, except where an existing privacy exposure
needed immediate remediation.

Excluded: bypassing publisher access controls, retaining full third-party bodies,
using confidential meeting/action/opportunity text in external searches, breaking-news
alerts, replacing the search provider, and active-active regional recovery.

## Pipeline and ownership

Discovery would produce candidates, followed by full fetch, normalization,
deterministic filtering, structured enrichment, quality validation, canonical
Markdown rendering, publication, and downstream KB/term/digest consumption.
Step Functions would orchestrate bounded independently retryable workers, not one
large Lambda. Contracts should be testable without AWS.

The proposal reused CrawlerStack for orchestration, AiStack for caller IAM, StorageStack
for private artifacts/backup, and GatewayStack plus the separately deployed research
container for internal-report changes. Role ownership and deployment dependencies
must be checked in current IaC; adding an environment variable alone cannot implement
candidate-level retry, error routing, or a publication state machine.

Only canonical Markdown would enter the KB bucket. Validated structured JSON and
staged Markdown would remain in the private assets bucket under
`knowledge-artifacts/`, outside CloudFront's media allowlist. Putting structured
artifacts in a KB data source without an inclusion filter could index duplicate
or private documents. The new prefix would not be a reason to make it public.

## Candidate, fetch, and evidence contracts

Candidates carried source identity, title/URL, discovery/publication time, query
class, and rank. Snippets were discovery/pre-filter signals only; a published item
needed a fetched body. Public aliases, industries, event terms, and configured AWS
services could form queries. Internal figures, codenames, actions, and conversation
text were excluded; query logs were to contain hashes/class/count/latency only.

News freshness defaulted to seven days, widening to 30 only after no accepted result.
Technical documents used content change rather than a publication cutoff. An explicit
custom URL bypassed freshness and the account-relevance threshold, but never fetch,
schema, evidence, or quality gates.

The proposed fetch controls were HTTP(S), at most five redirects, validated public
addresses on every hop, and connection to the validated IP to prevent DNS rebinding.
They included ten-second connection/read timeouts, two bounded transient retries,
compressed/decoded size limits, HTML/text types, and rejection of login/paywall/bot
challenge/JS-shell pages. News required 800 visible characters and four content blocks;
structured official references could pass with 500. Bodies stayed in process memory.
Rejected fetches created no published row, artifact, term suggestion, or digest item.

Canonicalization removed fragments/tracking parameters while preserving content
identifiers. A declared canonical URL had to resolve publicly on the same registrable
domain; source authority was checked against the final destination, preventing a
hostile page from borrowing another site's identity. Near-duplicate events needed
title-token Jaccard similarity of at least 0.75, a shared organization entity, and
publication within seven days. Additional fetched sources corroborated one event
rather than creating duplicate cards.

## Enrichment, model choice, and quality

The proposed versioned JSON contained an executive summary, facts with evidence IDs,
context, business/technical impact, risks/opportunities, justified AWS relevance,
meeting questions/actions, source links, terms, and optional diagrams. Evidence
excerpts were capped at 180 characters each and 1,000 collectively; normalized
article bodies were not retained. Canonical Markdown was generated by code, without
appending a raw article copy. Untrusted instructions/delimiters required neutralization
both before model use and before evidence entered the KB for later Q&A.

The selected planning configuration was Opus 5 through `us.anthropic.claude-opus-5`
with a us-west-2 client and `FINAL_MODEL_ID`. This was a proposed routing choice,
not the current crawler model or a verified residency guarantee. Internal research,
prep, and architecture paths required a deployment-controlled, default-off residency
approval gate or an approved regional alternative. Public content and internal
content could not silently share the same approval assumption. IAM would need the
selected profile and all permitted destination model ARNs, not an assumed single
regional model ARN.

Publication required a successful fetch, valid bounded schema, substantive summary
(original target: 600 Korean characters or equivalent), three facts including two
with evidence, evidence for every numeric claim, nonempty context/impact, and two
news questions/actions. Search-discovered relevance needed at least 0.75; explicit
custom URLs had only that relevance exception. Output links had to be safe, content
materially shorter than the source, and instruction-like payloads absent. One
constrained repair was allowed; a second failure rejected the candidate with no
snippet fallback. Versioned quality metadata recorded measurements and reason codes,
not raw bodies. Confidence scores alone were not evidence of correctness.

## Living technical knowledge

Only official/explicitly trusted AWS domains and organizations would establish
technical authority: documentation, announcements, blogs, architecture, solutions,
prescriptive guidance, Well-Architected material, samples, and workshops. Other
sources could remain news/research evidence.

A normalized-body SHA-256 `contentHash` would distinguish unchanged, new, and changed
content. Unchanged pages updated `lastCheckedAt` without model work; changed pages
replaced the current synthesis, incremented `docsUpdated`, and wrote compact change
records under `CRAWLER#__tech__/DOCCHANGE#` for 24 months. Historical full bodies and
application-level Markdown version history were excluded; S3 versions were recovery
material rather than a product history feature.

## Publication concurrency and recovery design

Three identities had different purposes: source/run candidate identity, stable
source-independent `docHash` from kind/canonical URL, and changing `contentHash`.
All publication state belonged to one `CRAWLERDOC#{docHash}/STATE` row. Per-source
`CRAWLER#{sourceId}/DOC#{docHash}` rows were read projections, never independent
claim targets. `primarySourceId` and technical `service` were immutable after the
first claim so later discovery could not move canonical storage ownership.

The intended state machine retained these safeguards:

1. Add each discovering source idempotently. If content was already published,
   repair that source's projection immediately; a source losing initial creation
   must rerun this path, not merely add itself after fan-out has finished.
2. Claim before model work. New rows used conditional creation; existing idle rows
   required `PUBLISHED` and a changed or absent published hash. Only the winner
   enriched, avoiding competing nondeterministic outputs for the same input.
3. Stage JSON/Markdown under a key containing both content hash and `claimedAt`
   attempt token. A reclaimed lease must not overwrite an older worker's artifact.
4. Quality pass moved `ENRICHING` to `PROMOTING` conditionally on both hash/token.
   Failure returned to idle `PUBLISHED` without changing the last published hash,
   recorded rejection, and applied a cooldown. Idle after first rejection was not
   proof that a published artifact existed.
5. Promotion copied the exact staged attempt to canonical KB Markdown and retained
   JSON, re-read claim state, and retried on changed generation. Commit to
   `PUBLISHED` matched both hash/token, cleared claim fields, and set
   `postPublishPending`.
6. Fan-out repaired every source projection. Ingestion completion handling cleared
   pending work only against the same published hash so a newer publish's work
   could not be hidden by an older completion.

Recovery treated states differently. Stale `ENRICHING` renewed the attempt token,
re-fetched the nonretained body, and verified its hash before repeating enrichment.
Stale `PROMOTING` reused the existing token and staged bytes; renewing it would
point to an artifact that never existed. `postPublishPending` was swept independently,
including after a publish commit, so crashes before fan-out/ingestion remained visible.
These cross-S3/DynamoDB operations still required adversarial concurrency/fault tests;
the design text was not proof of atomic publication.

Staging would expire current and noncurrent versions after seven days, with incomplete
multipart uploads aborted after one day. Promoted JSON was a separate retained prefix;
its metadata pointer must not expire with staging. Legacy snippet items would require
inventory, bounded resumable refetch/enrichment, replacement only after validation,
removal/hiding of failed items, and KB re-ingestion. Such destructive migration needs
its own authorized rollout, not automatic execution from this document.

## Dictionary automation

Trusted official terms could enter a system registry after deterministic checks.
Customer/product/industry/ambiguous terms required the relevant user's approval;
rejected normalized phrases stayed suppressed. Effective vocabulary combined active
system terms, that user's manual terms, and that user's approved suggestions.
Confidential terms must never leak into another user's vocabulary or ASR hints.

Proposed `TERM_SUGGESTION#` records and `VOCABULARY#EFFECTIVE` state tracked active
and pending names/hashes, status, errors, and retry count. Build a new hash-versioned
pending vocabulary rather than mutate the active one. Only confirmed READY output
could be atomically promoted; a failed build retained the last working vocabulary.
An unchanged failed hash still needed bounded retry, avoiding both permanent freeze
and an infinite build loop. Suggestions/approval/rejection/rebuild APIs were proposals.

## Daily digest

Users would explicitly opt into an accessible Account/Project plus subscribed
crawler sources; names alone would not infer a relationship. At most five event
groups would be ranked by relevance, freshness, authority, and corroboration.
One item would render deterministically; multiple public-source items could receive
a bounded synthesis. No accepted news meant no email.

Digest input excluded private meetings, actions, opportunity amounts, and internal
project names. Output needed substantive summaries/facts, significance, questions,
and ordinary source links, with sanitized responsive HTML and full plain text.
Tracking pixels, remote images, scripts, forms, and external stylesheets were excluded.

SES delivery proposed a deterministic user/local-date/scope key and conditional
`CLAIMED -> SENDING -> SENT|FAILED|BOUNCED|COMPLAINED|UNKNOWN` transitions.
`SENDING` had to commit before send. A stale pre-send claim could be reclaimed;
a crash at/after `SENDING` became UNKNOWN without automatic resend, trading possible
missed delivery for at-most-once intent. Terminal bounce/complaint feedback could
not be overwritten by a late SENT write. Correlated feedback would disable affected
subscriptions and surface an operational event. No email has been sent by this design.

## Research, preparation, and private content

Approval would snapshot structure, answers, scope, output type, selected resources,
and source policy into immutable `approvedPlan`. Workflow payloads would carry its
hash, with body loaded from storage. Access needed revalidation before execution
and each resource-touching stage; a snapshot was not a standing grant.

Research with internal context needed whole-query public-term allowlisting and
opaque references to URLs actually returned in the same search session. Raw
model-supplied fetch URLs and unvalidated redirect destinations could exfiltrate
content despite a safe search query. Report ID/owner would derive from verified job
context, not tool arguments; conditional writes would enforce the existing owner.
Tool manifests would measure source/word counts instead of trusting model totals.

Prep packets would structurally separate a public search/fetch call from an internal
synthesis call with no network tools. The internal call could use authorized prior
summaries/actions, agenda, constraints, and fixed public findings. Outputs included
objectives, changes, open actions, customer/technical context, risks, questions,
agenda, and evidence, optionally saved as an account `prep` document.

Architecture briefs would add current/target diagrams, requirements/assumptions,
alternatives, six-pillar analysis, low/base/high costs with dated sources,
reliability/SLIs/SLOs, idempotency/backpressure, observability, security, RPO/RTO,
migration/rollback, and validation. External capability/quota/price claims needed
official evidence or an explicit uncertainty label.

Internal reports were proposed under private `research/{userId}/{researchId}/`
keys, never `shared/research/`. Phase 1 was to move **all writers and readers**,
review/migrate the backlog, remove stale indexed vectors through ingestion, and
verify retrieval rather than infer removal from S3 alone. An IAM `research/*`
ceiling would not itself enforce per-user access. This privacy migration was
independent of Phase 4 feature delivery and is not proven complete today.

## Rendering, operations, cost, and recovery

Reuse the sanitized Markdown/strict Mermaid renderer. Rich cards would show facts,
provenance, and freshness; diagrams would support themes, zoom/reset/fullscreen/copy,
and stable responsive layout. A proposed server parse gate using the frontend's
Mermaid version allowed one repair before omitting an invalid diagram. Existing
client fallback did not prove server validation. The proposal to adopt Playwright
was a future convention change, not permission to override current lint/build rules.

Metrics covered discovery/fetch/rejections, freshness, model usage/repair/cost,
publication and ingestion, vocabulary age/failures, delivery feedback, and research
coverage. Proposed alerts included two failed search runs, 48-hour publication/
technical-check gaps, 24-hour ingestion lag, 30-minute vocabulary pending state,
delivery failure above 5% or any complaint, and a missing backup after 26 hours.
Transient work had bounded backoff; content rejection was terminal; ingestion failure
needed visible workflow failure.

Historical model planning used $5/$25 per million input/output tokens, with small,
base, and peak incremental monthly scenarios of $70-90, $230-280, and $770-900.
These are dated assumptions, not current prices or verified budgets; the original
scope excluded the existing vector-store baseline and separately contracted search
charges. Candidate/byte/round/token/dollar caps, one repair, hash-based skipping,
and a proposed pause at 120% of source budget would bound spend. No budget limiter
is implemented merely by stating a token estimate.

The proposed recovery target was same-Region RPO/RTO of 24 hours, not Region-outage
protection. It depended on PITR, a daily 35-day recovery point, versioned objects,
and a **new restored-table import/name mechanism**. The fixed table declaration did
not establish that cutover mechanism. KB versions' proposed 35-day retention remained
blocked on safe KnowledgeStack deployment; never deploy its staged teardown to add
that policy. Recovery would switch the validated table, restore object versions,
rebuild the vector index, reconcile vocabulary state, and restart schedules cautiously
without replaying old digest sends. Quarterly drills were to measure actual recovery.

## Validation, rollout, and current evidence

The planned phase gates were: full-fetch/schema/evidence for every visible news item
and private-report migration; content-hash refresh plus safe vocabulary promotion;
opt-in bounded digest delivery with feedback; then immutable-plan research and
source-grounded prep/architecture. Python/Go tests would cover rejected-input no-write,
SSRF/rebinding, publication races, failed builds, authorization/revocation, and SES
uncertainty. Infrastructure assertions and one-source canaries would precede expansion.
